Search is where most desk interactions begin. Use the box on the [Command Center](/onsite/command-center) or the **Search results** page. Enter at least three characters and press Enter. The type is detected automatically and shown in the box.

{/* SCREENSHOT: images/onsite/onsite-search-box.png | On the Command Center, type an email into the search box and press Enter. Capture the search box with its auto-detected label and the Clear button. */}

You can search by Ticket ID, email or name (attendee or purchaser), QR code content, order number, payment reference, top-up or spend reference, or wristband ID or UID.

## Read the results

Each card is a possible person. Pick the one actually at the desk.

{/* SCREENSHOT: images/onsite/onsite-search-results.png | Search for an email matching both a ticket holder and the purchaser of a group order so one amber card appears. Number Why it matched (1), the amber purchaser-match card (2), the ticket and entry details (3) and the Select / Resolve now buttons (4). */}

**Why it matched** matters most. A match on the ticket holder and a match on the purchaser are different people at the door. A purchaser match is highlighted in amber.

<Warning>
Don't admit someone because they bought a ticket. If the card says the match was the purchaser, the ticket belongs to someone else. Open that person instead.
</Warning>

- **Select** opens the full profile. See [Participants](/onsite/participants).
- **Resolve now** skips the profile and opens that ticket's guided resolution. See [Tickets & Entry](/onsite/tickets).

## When nothing matches

Below the results, **Nothing matched** (or **Not the right person?**) suggests what to try next. **Create unmatched case** records what the person told you so they aren't turned away with nothing. See [Service cases](/onsite/cases).

{/* SCREENSHOT: images/onsite/onsite-search-nothing-matched.png | Search for ZZZ999. Capture the Nothing matched panel with suggestions and the Create unmatched case button. */}

<Tip>
Don't tidy up what the guest gave you. The exact spelling and capitalization they presented is the only evidence the case has.
</Tip>
